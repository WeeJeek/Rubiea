# Rubiae name and logo redesign

Status: historical exploration, 2026-08-30. Superseded for current brand use by [EMPYRA 品牌名意义与叙事](empyra-brand-meaning.md) and [EMPYRA 网站品牌迁移记录](empyra-website-brand-migration.md); its candidate history remains preserved. Trademark and domain clearance for EMPYRA are still pending.

## Confirmed input

- Launch context: Netherlands, with Dutch and English customer-facing use.
- Brand: gemstone curation for women marking self-defined growth moments; tasteful, restrained, companionable, and evidence-led.
- `Velora` is rejected and must not return as a candidate.
- Dutch-language master-brand names are rejected because the user does not experience them as sufficiently premium. Dutch remains a customer-facing site language, not the naming direction.
- The first international longlist (`Alizaré`, `Erythéa`, `Alizora`, `Carminéa`, `Rhodéa`, `Rubéora`, `Kairéa`, `Feu Calme`, `Lueur de Soi`, and `Rare Light`) is rejected because it is too difficult to read. Future candidates must use no accents, no more than three syllables, and have an obvious first-reading pronunciation for Dutch and English speakers.
- Five supplied WhatsApp draft images were accessible and reviewed. They are concept drafts, not design instructions or approved assets.

## Draft diagnosis

`Vuurlijk`, `Gloed`, `Sol & Stil`, and `Rubi & Fier` all contain a potentially useful verbal idea, but their marks repeat the same category shorthand: literal faceted stones, rays or flames, red/gold gradients, warm cream, and generic high-contrast serif type. This reads more like a gemstone wholesaler, jewellery template, or ceremonial luxury badge than Rubiae's approved quiet editorial identity. `Gloed` has the calmest composition, but the name is already used in Dutch jewellery, including `Gloed Sieraden` and `Atelier Glans & Gloed`.

## Naming shortlist

The ten names below are rejected creative history, not approved names or trademark clearance.

| Rank | Candidate | Brand story | Preliminary concern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `Alizaré` | From alizarin and madder red; preserves the strongest truthful root behind `Rubiae` while sounding more finished. | Rejected: difficult to read. |
| 2 | `Erythéa` | Coined from Greek `erythros`, red; classical, restrained, and not limited to a literal ruby illustration. | Pronunciation must be taught once. |
| 3 | `Alizora` | Alizarin/madder red joined with the idea of dawn; suggests a new chapter without an empowerment slogan. | Existing cross-category creative use; exact/fuzzy clearance required. |
| 4 | `Carminéa` | From carmine, a deep cultivated red; warm, feminine, and suitable for an editorial identity. | Coined construction and accent friction. |
| 5 | `Rhodéa` | From Greek `rhodon`, rose; softer and more botanical than a literal gemstone name. | `Rhodea` already appears as a jewellery product name. |
| 6 | `Rubéora` | Ruby/red joined with aurora; connects stone, light, and a new beginning. | Already appears in gemstone product naming. |
| 7 | `Kairéa` | Inspired by Greek `kairos`, the meaningful or right moment; directly supports the growth-moment proposition. | Nearby company and product uses make clearance important. |
| 8 | `Feu Calme` | French for “calm fire”; carries forward the useful idea behind `Stilvuur` without using Dutch. | Two-word French pronunciation and phrase ownership need testing. |
| 9 | `Lueur de Soi` | French for a glow of one’s own; connects self-purchase with quiet light. | Longest and hardest candidate for Dutch/English speakers to spell. |
| 10 | `Rare Light` | Direct, international English; positions the stone as a quiet marker rather than a status symbol. | More descriptive and therefore potentially less ownable. |

`Stilvuur`, `Vuurlijk`, `Gloed`, `Sol & Stil`, and `Rubi & Fier` are no longer live master-brand candidates. `Pyréa`, `À Soi`, and `Velora` remain rejected.

## Recommendation

### Easy-reading reset

Preliminary search eliminated `Ruvia`, `Rova`, `Veya`, `Sola`, and `Elara` because exact jewellery brands already use them. The next five explorations are deliberately simple:

1. `Quiet Red` — two common English words; restrained and editorial.
2. `Still Ruby` — immediately readable and category-clear, but ruby-specific.
3. `Ruvana` — three syllables, no accent, visually related to `Rubiae`.
4. `Rubiva` — three syllables, keeps the ruby root without copying `Rubiae`.
5. `Ruvora` — three syllables, slightly stronger and more contemporary.

These five have only a preliminary web screen. Nearby names remain crowded, so none is approved or legally cleared.

Compare the five easy-reading directions against the current `Rubiae` benchmark. Do not draw final logos yet. Run Dutch/English first-reading, spoken-name, and spelling tests, then official exact/fuzzy searches for Nice Class 14 and relevant retail classes before approving one name.

## Logo direction after name approval

- Lead with a custom wordmark and one restrained secondary mark, not a gemstone illustration.
- One flat colour first; no gradients required for recognition.
- Preserve the approved cool paper/ink/deep-plum editorial world. Avoid black-red-gold, crowns, sunbursts, flames, and faceted clip-art unless a later concept proves a specific need.
- Require monochrome, reverse, favicon, website-header, invoice, packaging, and 16/24/32 px tests.
- Generated imagery remains `Needs Review`; final delivery requires clean editable vectors and font-license records.

## Screening boundary

Web search is only a preliminary collision screen. The [BOIP register](https://www.boip.int/en/trademarks-register) is the official public register for Benelux marks; [EUIPO eSearch](https://euipo.europa.eu/eSearch/) covers EUIPO records; [TMview](https://www.tmdn.org/tmview/) aggregates participating offices but states that it is not an official register and has no legal effect.
